# Week 1, day 2: vowels

## What today is for

Twelve vowel letters (i and y share a sound), six of them with a hook or hat that changes the sound entirely. German gives you most of the qualities; the danger is length. Vietnamese a is long, ă and â are short, and length is not decorative: mắt, mất, mát are three words.

| letters | sound | nearest German or Mandarin sound |
|---|---|---|
| a | long, open | Bahn |
| ă | same quality, very short, clipped | the a in "hat", cut even shorter |
| â | short, central, mouth half closed | the e in "bitte", or Mandarin 恩 without the n |
| e | open | Bett |
| ê | closed | Beet, Mandarin 欸 |
| i, y | same sound, spelling only | Mandarin 一 |
| o | open, rounded | offen |
| ô | closed, rounded | Ofen, Mandarin 喔 |
| ơ | unrounded ô, mouth relaxed | English "bird" without the r, shorter; Mandarin 婀 in 婀娜 |
| u | closed, rounded | Mandarin 烏 |
| ư | u with the lips spread as for i | no clean match in Mandarin or German; start from u and unround |

## Glides

ia, ua, ưa at the end of a syllable are i, u, ư gliding into a lazy ơ. Before a final consonant they are written iê, uô, ươ and sound the same. So bia and biết share the same first half, and mua and muốn do too.

## Saigon note

After o, ô, u a final ng or c closes the lips at the end (không, học), and một belongs there too because Saigon says its -t as -c. Day 4 drills that; today just notice it in một.

## Shadowing hints

Take the minimal sets slowly: three words, three lengths. Then the phrases at speed. If mất and mắt come out the same, you are lengthening ă; keep it as short as the voice does.
