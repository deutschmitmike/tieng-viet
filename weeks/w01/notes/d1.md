# Week 1, day 1: the five Saigon tones, greetings

## What today is for

Vietnamese spelling shows six tones. Saigon has five, because hỏi (ả) and ngã (ã) are pronounced the same. Your Mandarin gives you the categories; today is about calibrating the contours, which are not the Mandarin ones.

| tone | mark | Saigon contour | Mandarin comparison |
|---|---|---|---|
| ngang | none | mid, flat | like tone 1 but lower, 33 rather than 55 |
| huyền | à | low, falling gently, relaxed and slightly breathy | not tone 4: it starts low and falls softly, no force |
| sắc | á | rising | tone 2, nearly identical; on syllables ending in p, t, c, ch it is short and high |
| hỏi / ngã | ả, ã | dips, then rises, longer than the others | a full tone 3 (214), the one you use in isolation |
| nặng | ạ | low, short, cut off; on open syllables a tiny rise at the very end | no Mandarin equivalent; think of a tone 3 that never gets to rise |

What to listen for in the track: how short nặng is, how long hỏi is, and that huyền has no energy in it.

If the voice makes a break in the middle of ngã words (cũng, nữa, mã), that is the Northern realisation. Ignore it and say them exactly like the hỏi words.

## Pronouns today

anh = I (you, a man, to someone younger) and also you (when they address you). em = you (to someone younger) and I (when they speak). The tandems call you anh and themselves em; you do the reverse. That is the whole system for this week. tôi appears on day 3 as the neutral I; mình, bạn and the rest come in week 2.

## Shadowing hints

Greetings are said fast and low in Saigon. Chào em is almost one syllable long. Dạ is not "yes" in the German sense; it is what you say to show you are listening politely, and it starts almost every answer to someone you want to be polite to.
