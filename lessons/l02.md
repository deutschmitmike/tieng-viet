# Lesson 2: Particles, asking back, chatting
ids: s0122-s0153

== The little words at the end

Saigon speech lives on short words at the end of a sentence. They carry no content, only tone of voice, and they are what makes a sentence sound like a person instead of a textbook. Your Mandarin has the same idea: 吧、喔、耶、嗎.

| particle | what it does | close to |
|---|---|---|
| nha | softens a request or a plan | 喔、吧 |
| á | you know, I'm telling you | 喔、耶 |
| ha | asks the other person to agree | 對吧、耶 |
| luôn | totally; after a negative: at all | 超、完全 |
| nè | here, look: when you hand or send something | 給你、你看 |
| vậy | so, like that; softens a question (sao vậy?) | 呢、那樣 |
| đi | go ahead: turns a sentence into an invitation | 吧 |
| hả | checks something you just heard | 喔？、是喔 |
| hông | the yes-or-no question | 嗎 |
| ủa | at the start: huh, surprise | 咦 |

@@play s0131 s0135 s0133 s0142 s0136

Say them short and soft. A stressed particle sounds annoyed.

Hanoi uses other words for several of these (nhỉ for ha, này for nè, ơ for ủa, thế for vậy). You will read them; you say the Saigon ones.

== Asking back

The most useful sentences for a learner are the ones that keep a conversation going when you are lost. Tandems like being asked; it is the easiest way to get a correction.

@@play s0122 s0123 s0124 s0126 s0127

Em nói vậy là sao? means: what do you mean? Said gently it is a real question; said sharply it sounds annoyed. Smile when you say it.

== Reacting in a chat

Half of a chat is reacting. These are the reactions you will see most, and now you can send them too.

@@play s0139 s0140 s0141 s0128 s0130

Thiệt hông? really asks whether it is true; Thiệt hả? from lesson 1 mostly shows surprise. Trời, dữ vậy! is pure Saigon: wow, that much, seriously. Ờ ha means oh right, I forgot or I hadn't thought of that.

== hông and không

In Saigon, không is often said hông. The course keeps one simple rule for what you say:

- At the end of a yes-or-no question: hông. Em rảnh hông? Mình gọi video hông?
- In short fixed replies and in nghe hông kịp, ăn hông nổi: hông.
- In a full statement: không. Anh không hiểu. Anh không biết luôn.

The lesson 1 sentences keep their không; both are right, hông is simply more relaxed.

In chats you will see the short forms. You only need to read them: k or ko = không, and for hông also hong, hok, hem; dc or đc = được, r = rồi, j = gì, ms = mới, ntn = như thế nào. Write your own messages in full words for now; your tandems will read you better.

== Typing Vietnamese with Telex

Telex lets you type every Vietnamese letter on a normal keyboard. You type the base letters, and a few letter keys add the marks.

On the Mac: System Settings, Keyboard, Text Input, Edit, then the plus button, Vietnamese, and choose Telex (not Simple Telex, which does not turn a lone w into ư). Switch between keyboards with the globe key, or Control and Space on older keyboards.

On the iPhone: Settings, General, Keyboard, Keyboards, Add New Keyboard, Vietnamese, and pick Telex if it asks. Switch with the globe key on the keyboard.

| you type | you get |
|---|---|
| aa, ee, oo | â, ê, ô |
| aw, ow, uw (or w) | ă, ơ, ư |
| dd | đ |
| s, f, r, x, j after the word | sắc, huyền, hỏi, ngã, nặng |
| z after the word | removes the tone mark |

Examples: Vieetj gives Việt, nghiax gives nghĩa, khoer gives khỏe, dduwowcj gives được. Put the tone key at the end of the word; Telex places the mark on the right vowel by itself.

Two things to know. Telex may write khoẻ where the course writes khỏe: both are correct, only the place of the mark differs. And s, r and x are letters at the start of a word and tone keys after the vowel: rồi is typed rooif, xin is xin.

Practise in your chats: write one message a day with Telex instead of copying.
